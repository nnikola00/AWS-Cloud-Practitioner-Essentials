## 2. Five themes that run through everything

If you understand these, you can reason your way to answers you never memorized.

### Theme 1 — The Shared Responsibility Model

**AWS secures *of* the cloud. You secure *in* the cloud.** The line **moves by service**:

```
   EC2        you patch the OS, configure the firewall, secure the app
   RDS        AWS patches the DB engine; you handle users, network, data
   S3         AWS runs everything; you set policies, encryption, access
   Lambda     AWS runs everything up to the runtime; you own the code
   ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
   CONSTANT:  your DATA and WHO CAN REACH IT are always yours
```

Appears in: Storage (snapshots, encryption), Networking (SG/NACL are yours), Security
(the whole module), Governance (Config, Audit Manager).

### Theme 2 — The managed spectrum: control ⇄ effort

```
   MORE CONTROL ◄──────────────────────────────────────► LESS MANAGEMENT
   Outposts · EC2 · Lightsail · ECS/EKS on EC2 · Beanstalk · Fargate · Lambda
   EC2 instance store/EBS ····· EFS/FSx ····· S3
   RDS ····························· Aurora ····· DynamoDB
```

Same trade-off every time. **The question is never "which is best" — it's "how much control
does this workload actually need?"**

### Theme 3 — Scope and hierarchy

**Global → Region → AZ → subnet → resource.** Half of all "why doesn't this work" answers
are a scope mismatch (an EBS volume in the wrong AZ, an AMI in the wrong Region). See §3.

### Theme 4 — Resilience = two of everything, in two places

Multi-AZ subnets · Auto Scaling min ≥ 2 · RDS Multi-AZ · Aurora 6 copies/3 AZs ·
two Direct Connect links at two DX locations · cross-Region backups.
**"Dedicated" solves performance; only redundancy solves availability.**

### Theme 5 — Cost follows flexibility

```
   Pay as you go ──────► most flexible, highest unit price   (On-Demand)
   Commit 1-3 yrs ─────► ~72% off, locked in    (Savings Plans / Reserved)
   Accept interruption ► up to 90% off                            (Spot)
   Use more ───────────► tiered pricing, cheaper per GB
```

And the storage version: **cheaper to store = slower and/or costlier to retrieve.**

## 3. Scope — global vs. Region vs. AZ

One of the highest-yield tables in the whole course.

| Scope | Services / resources |
|---|---|
| **Global** | **IAM** · **Route 53** · **CloudFront** · **WAF** · **Shield** · **Organizations** · **Billing** · S3 **bucket names** (globally unique) |
| **Regional** | **S3 buckets** · **DynamoDB** · **Lambda** · **RDS/Aurora** · **VPC** · **SQS/SNS** · **ECS** · **EBS snapshots** · **AMIs** (copy to use elsewhere) |
| **Availability Zone** | **EC2 instances** · **EBS volumes** · **subnets** · **RDS instances** (the standby lives in another AZ) |

**Consequences you'll be tested on:**

- An **EBS volume attaches only to an instance in the same AZ**. Cross-AZ = snapshot → new volume.
- **AMIs and snapshots are Region-scoped** — copy them to use in another Region.
- **A VPC spans all AZs in its Region; a subnet lives in exactly one AZ.**
- **Nothing leaves a Region unless you move it.** That's the compliance guarantee.
- **IAM isn't in a Region** — there's no Region picker, and a user works everywhere.

## 4. The confusable pairs — the highest-value table here

Almost every scenario question is one of these. If you know the right-hand column cold,
you'll pass.

### Compute

| Pair | The distinction |
|---|---|
| **Instance store vs. EBS** | **Ephemeral** (dies on stop/terminate, survives reboot) vs. **persistent** |
| **ECS vs. EKS** | AWS-native, simpler vs. **Kubernetes**, portable, more complex |
| **Fargate vs. EC2 launch type** | Fargate replaces **EC2 as the capacity underneath** ECS/EKS — it is **not** an alternative to ECS/EKS |
| **Lambda vs. Fargate vs. EC2** | Event-driven, **15-min max** vs. containers, no servers vs. full control |
| **Beanstalk vs. Lambda** | Deploys your web app onto **real EC2 you still own** vs. **no servers at all** |
| **Dedicated Instances vs. Dedicated Hosts** | Isolated hardware vs. **the whole physical server, visible sockets/cores** (needed for **BYOL**) |
| **Scalability vs. elasticity** | *Can* grow vs. **grows and shrinks automatically** |

### Networking

| Pair | The distinction |
|---|---|
| **Security group vs. network ACL** | **Instance** level, **stateful**, **allow only** vs. **subnet** level, **stateless**, **allow + deny**, ordered |
| **Internet gateway vs. NAT gateway** | **Both directions**, public subnets vs. **outbound only**, for private subnets |
| **VPC vs. VPN vs. VGW** | The **boundary** vs. the **encrypted tunnel** vs. the **door** the tunnel attaches to |
| **Client VPN vs. Site-to-Site VPN** | Individual **people** vs. whole **networks/sites** |
| **PrivateLink vs. the VPNs** | **Never touches the public internet**; no IGW, NAT, or public IP needed |
| **Direct Connect vs. VPN** | **Dedicated physical circuit**, weeks to install, **not encrypted by default** vs. encrypted over the internet, live today |
| **CloudFront vs. Global Accelerator** | **Caches content** at the edge vs. **optimizes the network path** (no caching) |
| **Route 53 vs. CloudFront** | **DNS** — tells you *where to go* vs. **CDN** — *keeps a copy nearer you* |
| **Web ACL vs. network ACL** | **WAF**, HTTP layer vs. **VPC**, packet layer |

### Storage & databases

| Pair | The distinction |
|---|---|
| **Block vs. object vs. file** | One machine, a disk vs. whole items over HTTP vs. **many machines at once** |
| **EBS vs. EFS** | One instance, **one AZ**, provisioned vs. **many instances, multi-AZ, elastic** |
| **EFS vs. FSx** | **Linux/NFS** vs. **Windows / Lustre / ONTAP / OpenZFS** |
| **S3 Standard-IA vs. One Zone-IA** | 3+ AZs vs. **one AZ** (only for recreatable data) |
| **Glacier Instant vs. Flexible vs. Deep Archive** | **milliseconds** vs. **1–5 min / 5–12 h** vs. **12 hours** |
| **RDS vs. Aurora** | Six engines, lower cost vs. MySQL/PostgreSQL-compatible, **6 copies / 3 AZs**, auto-scaling to 128 TiB |
| **Multi-AZ vs. read replica** | **Availability**, synchronous, **not readable**, auto-failover vs. **performance**, asynchronous, readable |
| **DynamoDB vs. DocumentDB** | Key-value, serverless, extreme scale vs. **MongoDB-compatible** documents |
| **ElastiCache vs. a database** | A **temporary copy** in RAM — never the system of record |
| **Data lake vs. data warehouse** | **S3**, raw, schema on read vs. **Redshift**, structured, schema on write |

### Security, governance, cost

| Pair | The distinction |
|---|---|
| **Authentication vs. authorization** | **Who you are** vs. **what you may do** |
| **User vs. group vs. role** | Permanent identity vs. bundle of users vs. **temporary permissions you assume** |
| **Role: trust vs. permissions policy** | **Who may assume it** vs. **what they can then do** |
| **IAM policy vs. SCP** | **Grants** permissions vs. **sets the maximum** (a guardrail — never grants) |
| **Inspector vs. GuardDuty vs. Detective vs. Security Hub** | **Vulnerabilities (before)** vs. **active threats (during)** vs. **root cause (after)** vs. **all findings in one view** |
| **CloudWatch vs. CloudTrail vs. Config** | **How is it performing** vs. **who did what** vs. **how is it configured** |
| **CloudWatch vs. X-Ray** | A **component** is slow vs. **which part of one request** was slow |
| **Artifact Reports vs. Audit Manager** | **AWS's** audit evidence, for your auditors vs. evidence about **your** environment |
| **Organizations vs. Control Tower** | The raw structure vs. a **preconfigured best-practice landing zone** on top |
| **Trusted Advisor vs. WA Tool** | Continuous automated **checks** vs. a structured **review** with milestones |
| **Secrets Manager vs. Parameter Store** | Secrets with **automatic rotation** vs. general config, cheaper |
| **Budgets vs. Cost Explorer vs. Pricing Calculator** | Alert on the **future** vs. analyze the **past** vs. estimate **before you build** |
| **Marketplace vs. APN** | Buy **software** vs. find **partner companies/expertise** |
| **Solutions Architects vs. Professional Services** | **Advise** on design vs. **paid team that builds it with you** |

### AI/ML, analytics, migration, dev

| Pair | The distinction |
|---|---|
| **Polly vs. Transcribe** | Text **→ speech** vs. speech **→ text** |
| **Comprehend vs. Translate** | Extracts **meaning/sentiment** vs. changes the **language** |
| **Rekognition vs. Textract vs. Kendra** | What's **in an image/video** vs. text/forms/tables **in a document** vs. **answers across company content** |
| **Bedrock vs. SageMaker JumpStart** | Managed **API to FMs from many vendors** vs. a **hub inside SageMaker** you deploy from |
| **Q Business vs. Q Developer** | Company data and workflows vs. **code** |
| **Kinesis Data Streams vs. Firehose** | Real time, **you build the consumer** vs. near real time **delivery**, no code |
| **Athena vs. Redshift** | **Serverless**, queries S3 **in place**, pay per query vs. a **provisioned warehouse** for frequent heavy SQL |
| **Glue vs. EMR** | **Serverless ETL** vs. managed **Spark/Hadoop/Hive clusters** |
| **DMS vs. SCT** | Moves the **data** vs. converts the **schema** (heterogeneous needs both) |
| **DataSync vs. Transfer Family** | **Your** bulk transfers, scheduled vs. **others** sending you files over **SFTP/FTPS** |
| **Online vs. Snowball** | If it would take **more than ~a week** over the wire, **ship the device** |
| **CodeBuild vs. CodePipeline** | **One stage** (compile + test) vs. the **whole release pipeline** |
| **AppStream vs. WorkSpaces** | Streams **one application** vs. **an entire desktop** |

**Governance/Monitoring progression (4):** Secure → **Monitor (continuous)** →
**Audit (periodic)** → Comply

**Cost optimization order:** delete unused → right-size → turn off → tier storage →
**then** commit → Spot for the interruptible

## 9. Top 25 traps

1. **IAM defaults to deny**; an **explicit deny always wins** over any allow.
2. **A security group cannot block an IP** — allow rules only. Use a **network ACL**.
3. **Network ACLs are stateless** — allow return traffic **explicitly** (ephemeral ports).
4. **An SCP never grants permissions** — it caps them, and **admins can't override it**.
5. **A role is not a user.** Assuming one **replaces** your permissions; it doesn't add.
6. **Never put an access key on an EC2 instance** — attach a **role**.
7. **Instance store dies on stop/terminate**, survives **reboot**. EBS persists.
8. **An EBS volume is locked to one AZ** and one instance. Cross-AZ ⇒ snapshot.
9. **Snapshots are incremental**, but each restores a **full** volume; deleting one only
   removes blocks unique to it.
10. **S3 has no folders** — a flat namespace; slashes are characters in the key.
11. **Durability (11 nines) ≠ availability (99.99%).**
12. **Glacier Instant Retrieval is milliseconds** — not all Glacier is slow.
13. **Multi-AZ standby is not readable**; read replicas are, but don't auto-fail over.
14. **A cache is a copy**, never the system of record.
15. **CloudWatch = performance. CloudTrail = who did what. Config = configuration.**
16. **CloudTrail's free history is 90 days** — longer needs a trail to S3.
17. **Detective investigates; GuardDuty detects; Inspector scans for vulnerabilities.**
18. **Direct Connect is private but not encrypted** — add a VPN if you need both.
19. **One Direct Connect link is a single point of failure** — two, at two DX locations.
20. **CloudFront caches; Global Accelerator routes.** Different problems.
21. **Fargate doesn't replace ECS/EKS** — it replaces **EC2 underneath** them.
22. **Lambda's 15-minute cap** rules it out for long jobs → Batch/ECS/EC2.
23. **Inbound data transfer is free; outbound is charged.**
24. **Six Well-Architected pillars**, not five.
25. **Commit to Savings Plans last**, after cleanup and right-sizing — otherwise you lock
    in the waste.

**Why Global Infrastructure first:** Region/AZ/edge scope is assumed by every later module.
Reading it third means modules 1–2 land on an empty foundation.

**Why the two review days:** one module a day for twelve days means module 1 is three weeks
stale by the end. A mid-point review costs one day and saves the re-read.

### How to use each module

Each file has the same skeleton — use it deliberately:

1. **Read the tables**, not the prose. The prose is the courseware; the tables are the exam.
2. **Cover the right column and test yourself.** Recall beats re-reading, by a lot.
3. **The 🧒 ELI5 sections are the recall hooks.** When you blank on ECS vs. EKS, "two
   daycares" arrives faster than the definition, and drags the definition with it.
4. **Read the "traps" table last, and again the next morning.** Those are written as the
   misconception first, so they double as self-test questions.

### Exam mechanics

- **65 questions, 90 minutes** (~83 s each), multiple choice and multiple response.
- **Pass ≈ 700/1000**, scaled. **15 unscored** questions are mixed in — a weird question may
  simply not count.
- **No penalty for wrong answers — never leave a blank.**
- Four domains, roughly: **Cloud Concepts 24% · Security & Compliance 30% ·
  Technology & Services 34% · Billing, Pricing & Support 12%.**

**Note where the marks are: security and compliance is the largest single domain after
services.** Module 8 plus §4's security rows deserve disproportionate time — convenient,
given the day job.

### In the exam

1. **Read the last sentence of the question first.** It tells you what's actually being
   asked; the preceding paragraph is often scenery.
2. **Hunt the keyword** (§5). One word usually decides it: *MongoDB*, *graph*, *SFTP*,
   *rotate*, *without traversing the internet*.
3. **Eliminate on scope and category.** Two options are usually the wrong *kind* of service
   — a storage answer to a networking question. That leaves a coin flip at worst.
4. **Prefer the managed/AWS-native answer** when the question stresses *"minimal operational
   overhead"*, *"no servers to manage"*, or *"quickest to implement."* That phrasing is a
   deliberate signal.
5. **"MOST cost-effective" ≠ "cheapest."** It means cheapest **that still meets every stated
   requirement** — re-check the requirements before picking the bargain option.
6. **Flag and move.** Never spend three minutes on one question; the easy ones later are
   worth the same mark.

### The last hour before the exam

Read only: **§3** (scope), **§4** (confusable pairs), **§7** (numbers), **§9** (traps).
That's the whole course compressed into what's most likely to be tested and most easily
forgotten. Leave the modules alone at that point.
