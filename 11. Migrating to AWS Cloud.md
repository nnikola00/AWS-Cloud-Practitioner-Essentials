## 1. The three phases of migration

AWS divides migration into **three sequential phases**, each with services to support it.

```
   ┌──────────────┐      ┌──────────────┐      ┌────────────────────────┐
   │  1. ASSESS   │ ───► │ 2. MOBILIZE  │ ───► │ 3. MIGRATE & MODERNIZE │
   ├──────────────┤      ├──────────────┤      ├────────────────────────┤
   │ Build the    │      │ Prepare the  │      │ Use your strategy,     │
   │ BUSINESS     │      │ organization │      │ plan, and best         │
   │ CASE and     │      │ and MOBILIZE │      │ practices to migrate   │
   │ assess your  │      │ the resources│      │ and modernize          │
   │ READINESS    │      │ needed       │      │                        │
   ├──────────────┤      ├──────────────┤      ├────────────────────────┤
   │ Migration    │      │ Application  │      │ Application Migration  │
   │ Evaluator    │      │ Discovery    │      │ Service                │
   │              │      │ Service      │      │ AWS DMS                │
   │              │      │              │      │ DataSync · Transfer    │
   │              │      │ Migration    │      │ Family · Snow Family   │
   │              │      │ Hub ─────────┼──────┼──► (also used here)    │
   └──────────────┘      └──────────────┘      └────────────────────────┘
```

| Phase | What you do | Services |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Assess** | **Build the business case** for the migration and **assess your readiness** | **Migration Evaluator** |
| **2. Mobilize** | **Prepare the organization** and **mobilize the resources** needed | **AWS Application Discovery Service**, **AWS Migration Hub** |
| **3. Migrate and modernize** | Use your **strategy, plan, and best practices** to migrate and modernize | **AWS Application Migration Service**, **AWS DMS**. For transferring data: **DataSync**, **Transfer Family**, **Snow Family** |

**📌 Migration Hub spans two phases.** It helps you **mobilize**, but it's also the
**one-stop place to manage during migrate and modernize**. It's the thread running through
the whole process, not a one-phase tool.

### 🧒 ELI5 — moving house

- **Assess** — *should we even move, and can we afford it?* You work out what the new place
  costs, what you'd save, and whether you're actually ready.
- **Mobilize** — *what have we got, and what's connected to what?* You walk round the house
  writing down every item, noticing that the washing machine is plumbed into the wall and
  the TV needs the aerial. You book the van and tell everyone the date.
- **Migrate and modernize** — *moving day.* The van arrives. Some things go across exactly as
  they are; some get replaced with something better on the way.

The middle phase is the one people skip and the one that ruins moving day — because nobody
noticed what was connected to what.

## 2. AWS Cloud Adoption Framework (CAF)

When you migrate there are **critical planning tasks, many groups of stakeholders, and best
practices to consider**. It's easy to not even know where to start.

**The AWS CAF is a framework that brings AWS experience and best practices to companies
preparing to migrate.** It provides tools to **accelerate the migration journey, organize
resources, and align management** during the transition.

**Benefits:** **reduce business risk**, **improve sustainability and corporate
transparency**, **grow revenue** by creating new products and services during cloud
transformation, **reduce operational costs**, **increase productivity**, and **improve
customer experience**.

**Use cases:** migrate **technology** (legacy infrastructure and applications), migrate and
optimize **business processes and operations**, and even **create new business models**.

💡 **CAF is not a technical tool — it's an organizational one.** It exists because migrations
fail for people and process reasons far more often than technical ones. The servers move
fine; it's the team that didn't know who owns the new environment, or the finance department
still budgeting in capex, that stalls the project.

## 3. The six CAF perspectives

Six groups of stakeholders and parts of the business to consider.

| Perspective | What it ensures | Use it to | Common roles |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Business** | **IT aligns with business needs** and IT investments **link to key business results** | Create a **strong business case** for cloud adoption and **prioritize initiatives**; align business strategies and goals with IT strategies and goals | Business managers · Finance managers · Budget owners · Strategy stakeholders |
| **People** | Supports development of an **organization-wide change management strategy** | **Evaluate organizational structures and roles**, assess **new skill and process requirements**, and **identify gaps** — prioritizing training, staffing, and organizational changes | Human resources · Staffing · People managers |
| **Governance** | **Skills and processes to align IT strategy with business strategy**, maximizing business value and minimizing risk | Understand how to **update staff skills and processes** to maintain business governance in the cloud; **manage and measure cloud investments** to evaluate outcomes | CIO · Program managers · Enterprise architects · Business analysts · Portfolio managers |
| **Platform** | **Principles and patterns for implementing new solutions** in the cloud and **migrating on-premises workloads** | Use **architectural models** to understand and communicate the structure of IT systems and their relationships; **describe the target state environment in detail** | CTO · IT managers · Solutions architects |
| **Security** | The organization meets security objectives for **visibility, auditability, control, and agility** | **Structure the selection and implementation of security controls** that meet the organization's needs | **CISO** · IT security managers · **IT security analysts** |
| **Operations** | **Enable, run, use, operate, and recover IT workloads** to the level agreed with business stakeholders | Define how **day-to-day, quarter-to-quarter, and year-to-year** business is conducted; **define current operating procedures** and identify process changes and training needed | IT operations managers · IT support managers |

### 📌 The split the course doesn't draw, but everyone uses

The six divide cleanly into **two halves**, and it's the easiest way to remember them:

```
   BUSINESS CAPABILITIES          │   TECHNICAL CAPABILITIES
   (the people and the money)     │   (the systems and the running of them)
   ──────────────────────────     │   ─────────────────────────────────────
   1. Business    — why           │   4. Platform    — what we build
   2. People      — who           │   5. Security    — how we protect it
   3. Governance  — rules & value │   6. Operations  — how we run it
```

The first three are about the **organization**; the last three are about the **technology**.
A migration that only staffs the right-hand column is the classic failure mode.

🛡️ **Note where you'd sit:** the **Security perspective** explicitly lists **IT security
analysts** as a stakeholder role. Cloud migration isn't something security reviews at the
end — CAF puts it as one of six equal perspectives from the start.

### 🧒 ELI5 — the school play

Putting on a play needs six different groups thinking about six different things:

- **Business** — *is this play worth doing, and can we afford it?*
- **People** — *who's acting, who needs teaching, and who's nervous about it?*
- **Governance** — *what are the rules, and how do we know it went well?*
- **Platform** — *what does the set look like and how do we build it?*
- **Security** — *how do we keep the little ones safe on stage?*
- **Operations** — *who opens the hall, works the lights, and locks up afterwards?*

Build a beautiful set and forget who's locking up, and the play still goes wrong.

## 4. The seven migration strategies — the 7 Rs

The **seven common paths to the cloud**. Which you choose depends on **the complexity of
existing applications, business goals, time constraints, and available resources**.

> **Organizations often use a combination of these strategies across their application
> portfolio.** By considering each option and aligning it with specific applications, you
> create a **tailored migration plan** that maximizes benefits while **minimizing risks and
> disruptions**.

| # | Strategy | Also known as | What it means |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Relocate** | — | **Changing the hosting location** to the cloud. For applications **already VMs or containers** running on premises, moved as-is |
| **2** | **Rehost** | **Lift and shift** | **Moving applications without changes.** In a **large legacy migration** where a company wants to **scale quickly to meet a business case**, **the majority of applications are rehosted** |
| **3** | **Replatform** | **Lift, tinker, and shift** | **A few cloud optimizations** to realize a **tangible benefit**, **without changing the core architecture** |
| **4** | **Refactor** | **Re-architect** | **Reimagining how the application is architected and developed** using cloud-native features. Driven by a **strong business need** to add features, scale, or improve performance that's otherwise difficult |
| **5** | **Repurchase** | — | Moving from a **traditional license to a SaaS model**. *Example: migrating from a CRM system to new sales force software* |
| **6** | **Retain** | — | **Keeping applications in the source environment** — those needing **major refactoring first**, or work that can be **postponed** |
| **7** | **Retire** | — | **Removing applications that are no longer needed** |

### The effort/benefit trade-off

```
   LOW EFFORT                                                    HIGH EFFORT
   LOW CLOUD BENEFIT                                    HIGH CLOUD BENEFIT
   ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────►

   Retire     Retain     Relocate    Rehost    Replatform   Repurchase   Refactor
   (delete)   (leave     (move the   (lift &   (lift,       (swap for    (rebuild
              it)        VM)         shift)    tinker,      SaaS)        cloud-
                                               and shift)                native)
```

**📌 Two of the seven aren't migrations at all.** **Retire** and **Retain** are decisions
*not* to move — and they're often the most valuable outcomes of the assessment. A typical
portfolio review finds **10–20% of applications nobody actually uses**. Deleting them is the
cheapest migration there is.

### 🧒 ELI5 — moving house, item by item

You're moving to a new house. For every single thing you own, you pick one of seven:

1. **Relocate** — the wardrobe is already flat-packed, so it just goes in the van.
2. **Rehost** — the sofa goes across **exactly as it is**. No changes, no thought.
3. **Replatform** — the bed goes across, but you **buy a new mattress on the way**. Same bed,
   noticeably better.
4. **Refactor** — you **throw out the old kitchen entirely** and build a new one designed for
   the new house. Expensive and slow, but now it actually fits.
5. **Repurchase** — you don't move the washing machine; **you cancel it and start using the
   laundry service** down the road.
6. **Retain** — grandma's piano **stays at the old house for now**. It's too hard to move
   today.
7. **Retire** — the broken toaster in the cupboard goes **in the bin**. You'd forgotten you
   owned it.

Real moves are all seven at once. That's the point the course makes about combining
strategies.

💡 **Rehost is the most common by volume** — the course says so explicitly for large legacy
migrations. Speed first, optimize later, because you can't refactor 800 applications at once
and a data center lease doesn't wait.

## 5. Assess phase — Migration Evaluator

**A migration assessment service that helps you create a business case for AWS Cloud planning
and migration**, using a **data-driven approach**: analyzing your **current state**, **target
state**, and developing a **migration readiness plan with projected cloud costs**.

**Benefits:** **removes the guesswork**, provides **visibility into multiple cost-effective
migration scenarios**, and gives **insights on reusing existing software licensing**, which
can further reduce costs.

**Use cases:** conduct **broad-based discovery**, take a **snapshot of your on-premises
footprint** to **fine-tune licensing**, **view server dependencies**, and gain **visibility
into multiple migration scenarios**. Also **estimate and reduce cloud costs**.

💡 **The licensing insight is the sleeper feature.** Existing Microsoft or Oracle licenses
can often come with you under **BYOL** (`10. Pricing and Support.md`) — and that frequently
changes the business case more than the compute savings do.

**🧒 ELI5:** Migration Evaluator is the **surveyor who visits before you buy the house**. They
measure everything, work out what it'll really cost to live there, and hand you a report you
can show the bank. You haven't moved a single box yet.

## 6. Mobilize phase — Application Discovery Service and Migration Hub

### AWS Application Discovery Service

**Discovers on-premises server inventory and connections.** It gathers **configuration,
performance, and connection details for both servers and databases** to create a **detailed
migration plan**.

**Benefits:** a **comprehensive snapshot of your on-premises inventory**, the ability to
**integrate discovery data with other services like Migration Hub**, and **protection for the
data it collects**.

**Use cases:** **conduct discovery and inventory**, **map the connections and dependencies**,
and **generate a migration plan**.

**📌 "Dependencies" is the whole reason this service exists.** The course illustrates it with
a mover loading a van saying *"I thought I was only moving the application"* — while the
compute resource says *"Won't work without me"* and the database says *"Where you go, I go!"*

That's the single most common migration failure: you move one application, and it breaks
because it quietly depended on three things nobody documented. Discovery finds the wires
before you pull them.

**🧒 ELI5:** the **person who crawls behind the TV and writes down every cable** before you
unplug anything. Otherwise you get to the new house with a TV, a games console, and no idea
which cable went where — or that the internet box was doing something important.

### AWS Migration Hub

**A centralized hub taking you from discovery, assessment, planning, and execution.** It
provides **tools, guidance, and automated recommendations** to **collaborate with your team
and track your migration**.

**Benefits:** **one location** for your migration, **expert guidance in the form of
prescriptive journey templates**, and **cost savings — there is no charge to use Migration
Hub**.

**Use cases:** **migration assessment and planning**, **migration completion and
collaboration with teams**, and **modernization efforts** like **fast-tracking application
refactoring**.

💡 **Free, and it spans the whole journey.** Note the pattern from other modules: AWS often
doesn't charge for the *management* layer (Migration Hub, Security Hub, Organizations,
CloudFormation) — you pay for the resources underneath.

**🧒 ELI5:** the **big whiteboard in the hall on moving day**, listing every room, who's
packing it, and whether it's arrived yet. Anyone can walk past and see how the move is going
— which is exactly the coworker in the course's picture asking "how's the migration going?"
and getting a real answer.

## 7. Migrate and modernize — Application Migration Service

**A tool to move and improve your on-premises and cloud-based applications**, helping
customers **streamline, expedite, and reduce the cost of migrating and modernizing**.

**Benefits:** supports migration **from any source infrastructure running a supported OS**,
makes it possible to **modernize applications during migration**, lets you **maintain normal
business operations during the replication process**, and **reduces costs by using one tool
for a wide range of applications**.

**Use cases:** **on-premises applications on physical servers or infrastructure**,
**cloud-based applications**, **moving between AWS Regions**, and **modernizing
applications**.

**📌 "Maintain normal business operations during replication" is the key capability.** The
service **continuously replicates** your servers into AWS while they keep running. The actual
cutover is then a short, planned switch rather than days of downtime — which is what makes
large **rehost** migrations feasible at all.

**🧒 ELI5:** imagine a **magic van that copies your furniture to the new house while you're
still using it**. The sofa stays in your living room and a perfect copy appears at the new
place, kept in sync. When you're ready you just **walk out of one house and into the other** —
and everything's already there.

## 8. Migrating databases — DMS and SCT

Migrating a database is **an opportunity to redesign and improve your architecture**. Because
legacy systems and requirements change, consider whether to move to an **AWS managed database
service** or an **open source database to reduce licensing costs**.

### The two kinds of database migration

| | **Homogeneous** | **Heterogeneous** |
|---|---|---|
| Meaning | **Same database engine** to the same engine | **One type of database to a different type** |
| Example | Oracle → Oracle · MySQL → MySQL on RDS | **Oracle → Aurora PostgreSQL** · SQL Server → MySQL |
| Difficulty | **Fairly simple** | **More complex — might require changes in your application** |
| Tools | **AWS DMS** alone | **AWS SCT first, then AWS DMS** |

### AWS Database Migration Service (DMS)

**Quickly and securely migrate databases and perform ongoing data replication** for **live
databases and data warehouses**. It provides a way to **plan, assess, convert, and migrate
databases — even data warehouses — in one central tool**.

**Benefits:** **high availability and low downtime** during migration, support for **both
homogeneous and heterogeneous** migrations, and the ability to **migrate terabyte-sized
databases at low cost**.

**Use cases:** **move to managed databases**, **remove licensing costs**, **replicate ongoing
changes**, and **improve integration with data lakes**.

### AWS Schema Conversion Tool (SCT)

When source and target engines differ, you must consider **how to recreate existing resources
like the database schema** in the target — and doing it manually is slow.

> **A schema defines the structure and organization of data inside the database** — a
> blueprint for **table structures, field types, and relationships between items**.

**AWS SCT converts database schemas and code objects** — **stored procedures, views, and
functions** — **from one database engine to another**. It can even **estimate how big an
effort a conversion is**, which helps with planning.

**Benefits:** **automates schema analysis, recommendations, and conversion at scale**;
**compatible with popular databases and analytics services** as source and target; **saves
weeks or months of manual time**.

**Use cases:** **move from commercial to open source databases**, **migrate large data
warehouse workloads**, and **modernize or update schemas in place**.

### 📌 How they work together

```
   HOMOGENEOUS (MySQL → MySQL)
   ───────────────────────────
   Source DB ──────── AWS DMS ────────► Target DB
                   (moves the data)
                   Schema already fits.


   HETEROGENEOUS (Oracle → Aurora PostgreSQL)
   ──────────────────────────────────────────
   Source DB ─── AWS SCT ───► converts the SCHEMA and code objects
                              (tables, types, stored procedures,
                               views, functions) to the new dialect
                   │
                   ▼
             ─── AWS DMS ───► then moves the DATA
                              (with ongoing replication, so the
                               source stays live until cutover)
```

**SCT moves the *blueprint*. DMS moves the *contents*.** That one line is the whole
distinction, and it's exactly what the exam asks.

### 🧒 ELI5 — moving to a house with different-shaped rooms

**Homogeneous** is moving into an **identical house** next door. Every room is the same shape,
so the furniture just goes straight in. **One trip, one van.** *(DMS.)*

**Heterogeneous** is moving into a house with **completely different rooms** — no space where
the wardrobe went, and the kitchen is on the other side. Before you move anything, someone
has to **redraw the plan**: what goes where, what needs rebuilding, what doesn't fit at all.
*(That's SCT.)* **Then** the van brings the stuff. *(That's DMS.)*

And the clever part about the van: it keeps **copying things across while you still live in
the old house**, so you're never without a bed.

## 9. Transferring data online

When migrating data, keep in mind: **security** (will it get there safely), **data
validation** (will it get there in one piece), **scheduling** (when is the best time), and
**bandwidth requirements**.

> **For the majority of data migration workloads, AWS DataSync will do the job.**

### AWS DataSync

**Specifically designed for automating and accelerating data transfer.** It **simplifies and
accelerates moving large amounts of data between on-premises storage and AWS storage services
like Amazon S3**, automating **running instances, encryption, and network optimization**.

**Benefits:** **streamlines and accelerates secure data migrations**; manages workloads with
**bandwidth throttling, migration scheduling, task filtering, and task reporting**; provides
**rapid data replication**.

**Use cases:** **migrate your data**, **archive cold data**, and **manage hybrid data
workflows**.

💡 **Bandwidth throttling matters more than it sounds.** Saturating the office internet
connection with a migration during business hours is a self-inflicted outage. Throttling plus
scheduling means the transfer runs overnight at a rate that leaves the business working.

### AWS Transfer Family

**Fully managed support for file transfers into and out of Amazon S3** and **Amazon EFS**,
over **FTP, SFTP, FTPS, and other protocols**.

> **A transfer protocol is a set of rules or standards that govern the way data is moved from
> one location to another.**

**Benefits:** **simplifies setting up and managing file transfers**, **reduces the need for
complex infrastructure management**, provides **secure transfer with encryption and
authentication** for **data integrity and confidentiality**, and is **built to scale**.

**Use cases:** **modernize and manage file transfers**, **simplify data sharing with your
workforce and partners**, and **integrate transactional business data into a unified data
lake**.

**📌 DataSync vs. Transfer Family — the distinction:** **DataSync is for *you* moving *your*
data** in bulk, on a schedule. **Transfer Family is for *other people* sending you files**
over the protocols their systems already speak. If a question mentions **SFTP** or
**partners/vendors uploading files**, it's Transfer Family.

### AWS Direct Connect

From the networking module: **a dedicated private connection between your network and your
VPC**. With the bandwidth of a dedicated connection, it's **a fast, reliable, and secure way
to transfer data or files** when migrating.

**Benefits:** **reduces network costs** and **increases available bandwidth**.

💡 Worth remembering from `Networking.md`: Direct Connect takes **weeks** to provision because
someone lays a physical circuit. Great for a long migration or a permanent hybrid link —
useless if you need to move data next Tuesday.

## 10. Transferring data offline — the Snow Family

### Snowball Edge Storage Optimized devices

**A great solution for offline data migration where connecting to the internet might not be
an option.** These devices deliver **high-performance NVMe storage**, making it possible to
**simplify multi-petabyte data migrations** from on-premises locations to AWS.

**Benefits:** **better compute performance and larger storage capacity**, with **gigabytes of
data per second** for **data migration workloads with offline requirements**.

**Use cases:** **data migration when offline migration is required**, and **edge computing
when a secure, rugged device is needed**.

### 🧒 ELI5 — posting the hard drive

You need to give your friend 500 films. Your internet is slow — uploading them would take
**three months**.

So instead you **copy them onto a hard drive and post it.** It arrives in two days.

That's Snowball. AWS **physically ships you a rugged, encrypted storage device**. You fill it
up, ship it back, and they load it into S3 for you. It sounds primitive, and for large
enough datasets **it is genuinely faster than any internet connection**.

> *"Never underestimate the bandwidth of a van full of hard drives."* — the actual engineering
> principle here.

💡 **The second use case is easy to miss:** Snowball Edge devices are also for **edge
computing** — running compute on a **secure, rugged device** somewhere with no reliable
connectivity. A ship, a mine, a military deployment, a remote factory. It's not only a
moving box.

## 11. Choosing a data transfer method

### The decision

```
 Can the data get there over the network in an acceptable time?
 │
 ├── NO — too much data, or no/poor connectivity
 │      └────────────────────────────────► AWS Snow Family (Snowball Edge)
 │                                          ship the device
 │
 └── YES — online transfer
        │
        ├── Bulk migration of MY data, scheduled,
        │   on-prem storage → S3/EFS/FSx ──────► AWS DataSync
        │
        ├── OTHER PEOPLE send files to me over
        │   SFTP / FTPS / FTP ─────────────────► AWS Transfer Family
        │
        ├── A DATABASE (live, with ongoing
        │   replication) ──────────────────────► AWS DMS
        │      └── different engine? add ──────► AWS SCT first
        │
        └── Need dedicated, consistent bandwidth
            for a long or permanent link ──────► AWS Direct Connect
```

### The rule of thumb nobody writes down

Work out how long the transfer would actually take:

```
   Days ≈ (Terabytes × 8,000) ÷ (Mbps × 0.7) ÷ 86,400
                                      ↑
                          realistic usable share of the link
```

| Data | Over 100 Mbps | Over 1 Gbps |
|---|---|---|
| **1 TB** | ~1.3 days | ~3 hours |
| **10 TB** | ~13 days | ~1.3 days |
| **100 TB** | **~4 months** | ~13 days |
| **1 PB** | **~3 years** | **~4 months** |

**The industry heuristic: if the online transfer would take more than about a week, ship a
device.** That's the whole decision, and it's why the Snow Family exists.

### Migration services cheat sheet

| Service | Phase | One line |
|---|---|---|
| **Migration Evaluator** | Assess | Builds the **business case** with projected costs and licensing insights |
| **Application Discovery Service** | Mobilize | Discovers **inventory, configuration, performance, and dependencies** |
| **Migration Hub** | Mobilize → Migrate | **Free** central place to plan, track, and manage the whole migration |
| **Application Migration Service** | Migrate | **Lift-and-shift** servers with continuous replication and minimal downtime |
| **AWS DMS** | Migrate | Migrates **databases** with low downtime; homogeneous **and** heterogeneous |
| **AWS SCT** | Migrate | Converts the **schema and code objects** between different engines |
| **AWS DataSync** | Migrate | Automated, accelerated **bulk data transfer** on-prem → AWS |
| **AWS Transfer Family** | Migrate | Managed **SFTP/FTPS/FTP** endpoints into S3 and EFS |
| **AWS Direct Connect** | Migrate | **Dedicated private circuit** for consistent bandwidth |
| **Snowball Edge** | Migrate | **Physical device** for offline/petabyte-scale transfer and edge compute |

## 12. Quick recap + common exam traps

### Recap

- **Three phases:** **Assess** (business case, readiness — Migration Evaluator) → **Mobilize**
  (prepare and discover — Application Discovery Service, Migration Hub) → **Migrate and
  modernize** (Application Migration Service, DMS, DataSync, Transfer Family, Snow Family).
  **Migration Hub spans the last two.**
- **AWS CAF** brings AWS experience and best practices to migration planning — an
  **organizational** framework, not a technical one.
- **Six CAF perspectives:** **Business, People, Governance** (business capabilities) and
  **Platform, Security, Operations** (technical capabilities).
- **Seven migration strategies:** **Relocate, Rehost** (lift and shift), **Replatform** (lift,
  tinker, and shift), **Refactor** (re-architect), **Repurchase** (to SaaS), **Retain**,
  **Retire**. Most portfolios use a **combination**; **rehost dominates by volume** in large
  legacy migrations.
- **Homogeneous** migration (same engine) = **DMS**. **Heterogeneous** (different engine) =
  **SCT for the schema, then DMS for the data**.
- **Online transfer:** **DataSync** (bulk, yours, scheduled), **Transfer Family**
  (SFTP/FTPS/FTP, others sending to you), **Direct Connect** (dedicated bandwidth).
- **Offline transfer:** **Snowball Edge Storage Optimized** — multi-petabyte migration where
  the internet isn't an option, plus **edge computing** on a rugged device.

### Traps

| Trap | The truth |
|---|---|
| "Rehost and replatform are the same" | **Rehost = no changes** (lift and shift). **Replatform = a few optimizations** without changing the core architecture (lift, **tinker**, and shift) |
| "Refactor just means moving to containers" | **Refactor = re-architecting** using cloud-native features, driven by a strong business need to add features, scale, or improve performance |
| "All seven strategies involve moving something" | **Retain** (leave it) and **Retire** (delete it) are decisions **not** to migrate |
| "Repurchase means buying more AWS services" | It means moving **from a traditional license to SaaS** — e.g. replacing a self-hosted CRM with a SaaS one |
| "DMS converts the schema too" | **DMS moves the data.** **SCT converts the schema and code objects.** Heterogeneous migrations need **both** |
| "Heterogeneous migration is just as easy" | It's **more complex** and **might require changes in your application** |
| "Migration Hub costs extra" | **No charge** to use it |
| "Application Discovery Service migrates servers" | It **discovers** inventory, configuration, performance, and **dependencies**. **Application Migration Service** does the moving |
| "DataSync and Transfer Family are interchangeable" | **DataSync** = your bulk transfers, scheduled and throttled. **Transfer Family** = managed **SFTP/FTPS/FTP** endpoints, usually for partners uploading to you |
| "Snowball is only for when you have no internet" | Also for when you *have* internet but the transfer would take **months** — and for **edge computing** on a rugged device |
| "CAF is a technical migration tool" | It's an **organizational framework** covering stakeholders, skills, and process — three of its six perspectives aren't technical at all |
| "You pick one migration strategy for the whole portfolio" | Organizations **combine strategies**, choosing per application |
